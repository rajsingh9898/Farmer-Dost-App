# Farmer Dost App — Competitor Research

Researched 4 real products that solve a similar problem: verifying product authenticity via smartphone scan, with a focus on agriculture-relevant examples.

---

## 1. Bayer Seal Scan App / FarmRise (Bayer Crop Science, India)

**What it does:** Lets Indian farmers scan the QR code on a Bayer "Safety Seal" to confirm a Bayer Crop Science product helps Indian farmers check the authenticity of Bayer Crop Science products by scanning the QR code on the Bayer Safety Seal, providing instant feedback on the product's originality.

**Does well:**
- Directly targets the exact use case Farmer Dost is solving — farmer-facing, agri-input authentication
- Simple, single-purpose flow: scan → instant result
- Free, simple, quick, and reliable — low friction for farmers

**Does poorly:**
- Single-brand only — it verifies Bayer products exclusively, so it's useless for the dozens of other fertilizer/pesticide brands a farmer might buy
- No cross-brand database, no dealer trust ratings, no community reporting feature
- No advisory or crop-support features beyond authentication

**Takeaway for Farmer Dost:** This validates that farmers already have some exposure to "scan to verify" apps — but the market gap is a **multi-brand, independent** verifier, not another single-company tool.

---

## 2. Checko (IIT Kanpur-incubated startup)

**What it does:** Uses a 3D signature/pattern authentication technology; farmers scan with the free Checko app to check authenticity within seconds. Chambal Fertilisers, one of India's leading agrochemical players, adopted the technology to secure its crop protection products, and the founder notes the system rebuilt trust with end users, particularly farmers, and strengthened brand credibility within the rural supply chain.

**Does well:**
- Purpose-built for the Indian agri-input counterfeit problem, with real fertilizer-company adoption (Chambal)
- Technology is harder to clone than a standard printed QR code, addressing a real weakness of basic QR systems
- Strong storytelling around farmer trust, not just brand protection

**Does poorly:**
- Still a manufacturer-driven, opt-in system — only works for brands that adopt Checko's labels, so coverage is partial
- No visible farmer-facing features beyond verification (no advisory, no dealer reporting visible in public info)
- Adoption depends entirely on manufacturers paying to integrate it, which is slow and leaves smaller/regional brands (and easiest counterfeit targets) uncovered

**Takeaway for Farmer Dost:** Confirms real market demand and precedent — but shows the weakness of manufacturer-only rollouts. An independent farmer-first app with a broader database (or dealer/government-fed data) could cover the gap Checko leaves.

---

## 3. Scantrust

**What it does:** A global, general-purpose anti-counterfeit app. Scantrust allows you to verify if a product is real or counterfeit by scanning a secure Scantrust QR code, seeing the product's origin, and reporting questionable products directly to the brand in real time.

**Does well:**
- Includes a **report-to-brand** feature, similar to Farmer Dost's planned "report fake" functionality
- Shows rich product info beyond genuine/fake (origin, production date, shipping info)
- Not sector-locked — works across many industries, showing the model is flexible

**Does poorly:**
- Generic, not farmer-specific — no agricultural context, language options for rural India, or offline support are mentioned
- Reporting goes to the brand, not to a farmer-protecting authority — a report might just protect the manufacturer's reputation, not directly help other farmers avoid the same fake seller
- No crop advisory, dealer trust score, or community layer

**Takeaway for Farmer Dost:** The reporting mechanism is a good pattern to borrow, but Farmer Dost should route reports toward farmer protection (flagging dealers, alerting nearby farmers) rather than just brand protection.

---

## 4. Chekkit App

**What it does:** The easiest way to identify fake products and check authenticity by scanning QR codes or barcodes, with an added twist: users can report counterfeit products and win rewards such as tokens, airtime, and cash prizes for verifying products.

**Does well:**
- Gamifies verification with rewards — a clever way to drive habitual usage, which could be adapted (e.g. small incentives for farmers who report fakes)
- Works without downloading the app at all via a website — useful accessibility pattern for farmers on very basic phones
- Combines both scanning and reporting in one simple loop

**Does poorly:**
- Only works on products carrying Chekkit's own scratch-off sticker labels — same single-vendor lock-in problem as Bayer's app, just from a different company
- Reward-driven model could invite gaming/fraud if not carefully designed (fake reports for rewards)
- Not agriculture-specific; general consumer goods focus

**Takeaway for Farmer Dost:** The web-based no-download option is worth strongly considering for farmers with basic phones or limited storage. The rewards idea is interesting but risky — worth testing carefully rather than copying directly.

## Summary: What This Means for Farmer Dost's Positioning

| Gap in existing solutions | How Farmer Dost can differentiate |
|---|---|
| Every real competitor is single-brand or manufacturer-locked | Build an independent, multi-brand verification database from the start |
| Reporting mechanisms protect brands, not farmers | Route "report fake" data toward dealer accountability and farmer alerts |
| None are agriculture-and-India-specific with local language/voice support | Design natively for Punjab's farmers — regional language, low-literacy-friendly UI, offline support |
| No app combines authentication with crop advisory | Layering basic advisory features later (Phase 5+) is a genuine differentiator, not just a nice-to-have |

This research confirms there's real precedent for this idea working (Bayer, Checko), a real problem being addressed industry-wide, and a clear, defensible gap Farmer Dost can fill: an **independent, farmer-first, multi-brand verification app** built for the Indian small-farmer context specifically.
